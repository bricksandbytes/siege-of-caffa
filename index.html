Dungeons & Dragons (D&D) is a tabletop game where you describe what your character does, roll dice to see what happens, and tell a story together.

One person is the Game Master (GM). Everyone else plays a character. The GM describes the world. You describe your actions. Dice decide uncertain outcomes.

Plague Walkers of Caffa

Lord of the Rings (LOTR) meets The Walking Dead (TWD).

The siege has begun again.

Only two years have passed since the last army of the Golden Horde withdrew from the walls. Now Jani Beg Khan, ruler of the Golden Horde, has returned—and this time he intends to finish what he started.

Inside the walls of Caffa, the great Genoese port on the Black Sea, the gates are closing.

But the people pouring through them have not stopped.

Merchants fleeing the war. Families escaping the countryside. Traders from Tana. Sailors, craftsmen, soldiers, refugees, pilgrims, and opportunists. Greeks. Armenians. Jews of the diaspora. Genoese. Venetians. Tatars. People from across the Black Sea and the trade routes beyond it.

They seek safety behind Caffa's walls.

Instead, they bring a city already under immense pressure to the breaking point.

Caffa is wealthy enough to attract the world—and crowded enough that every mouth matters, every warehouse matters, and every faction has something to lose.

The city's Genoese authorities struggle to maintain order and defend the walls. The Consul of Caffa and Gazaria must hold together a population divided by language, faith, wealth, and allegiance while an army gathers outside.

Far away in Genoa, Doge Giovanni I di Murta watches the crisis through reports, merchants, captains, and letters. Caffa is more than a distant colony.

It is a gateway to the wealth of the Black Sea.

And its fortunes are tied to the merchants, financiers, and political interests of Genoa—including the growing power of the Casa di San Giorgio, the Bank of Saint George.

Inside the city, everyone has an agenda.

Genoese merchants defend their privileges and fortunes.

Greek communities navigate between competing powers.

Armenian merchants and families maintain networks reaching deep into the East.

Jewish communities of the diaspora trade, negotiate, and survive between worlds.

Venetians have their own interests in a city dominated by their rivals.

And behind them all stand the enormous commercial networks that make Caffa one of the most important ports in the world.

Outside the walls waits Jani Beg Khan and the armies of the Golden Horde.

Inside waits hunger, politics, disease, ambition—and opportunity.

You have arrived at the worst possible moment.

Or perhaps the most important.

The siege will change Caffa.

It may change Genoa.

It may change Europe.

And somewhere among the crowded streets, fortified walls, merchant houses, churches, synagogues, markets, warehouses, and refugee camps, history is waiting for you to act.
