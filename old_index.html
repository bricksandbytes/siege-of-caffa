🛡️ The Siege of Caffa (1345–1347)

An Experiential Humanities & Tabletop Roleplaying Curriculum

Welcome to The Siege of Caffa, an immersive educational experience that bridges medieval history, critical thinking, literature, and game-based learning. Step inside the walled Black Sea trading port of Caffa in 1345. Trapped behind city walls as Mongol armies encircle the city, students do not merely read about the past—they live through it, navigate its moral dilemmas, and actively shape alternate historical realities.

🏛️ What is Experiential Humanities?

Transforming students from passive observers of history into active, critical decision-makers.

🧠

Deep Historical Empathy

Understanding the panic, socio-economic pressures, and public health dilemmas experienced by people across 14th-century Eurasia.

🔀

Critical Counterfactual Reasoning

Exploring how micro-decisions influence macro-historical trajectories across epidemiology, trade routes, and military tactics.

🗣️

Strategic Communication

Negotiating with rival city factions, coordinating defenses, and managing urban panic under real-time high-stakes pressure.

⏳ Do Students Create Alternate Timelines?

Yes. Beginning anchored in historical reality (1345), student decisions generate branching alternate histories.

📜 Historical Reality (The Baseline)

In 1347, Khan Jani Beg catapults plague-infected corpses over Caffa’s walls. Terrified Genoese merchants flee on ships, carrying the Yersinia pestis bacterium to Southern Europe and igniting the Black Death.

🔀 Student-Driven Divergence

Localized quests, sanitation efforts, and political maneuvers alter the city's fate:

  • Can strategic quarantine protocols delay or mitigate the plague’s arrival in Western Europe?
  • Can party diplomacy secure supply lines, changing how long Caffa holds out under siege?
  • Will their choices prevent or accelerate the collapse of Mediterranean trade networks?
